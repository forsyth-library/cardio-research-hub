    Build your search

    Databases don't understand questions. They match words. Turn your question into a search in three steps.

    1. Key ideas we spotted. Click one to use it:
    2. Pick 2–3 key ideas, and add other words for each

      Each idea is something every article must be about. Other words catch articles that use different vocabulary, like hypertension for “high blood pressure.”

    3. AND means every idea must appear, which narrows results. OR means any of the words can appear, which widens them. Quotation marks keep a phrase together. You can edit the search directly.

      Too many results? Add another key idea. Too few? Remove an idea, or add more other words.

    Search tips

    • Search key terms, not sentences. For “How does sleep affect blood pressure in teens?”, search sleep blood pressure adolescents.
    • Too many results? Add a term (adolescents, exercise) or choose a more specific word.
    • Too few? Remove a term, or try a synonym: hypertension for “high blood pressure,” myocardial infarction for “heart attack.”
    • Can't get the full article? Ask the library before you give up on it.

    Start a PBAT

    Good research questions come from reading first. Work through these five stages in order. Your progress saves in this browser, and the sources and question you record also appear in My sources.

      Clear your guide progress? Your question and sources stay in My sources.

      Sharpen your question

      A strong PBAT question can't be answered by one fact. It asks how or why, and you can actually find evidence for it.

      ProblemWeakStronger
      Too broadWhat is heart disease?How does LDL oxidation contribute to plaque formation in coronary arteries?
      Yes/noIs exercise good for your heart?How does aerobic training change stroke volume, and why?
      Can't be researchedWill heart disease be cured by 2040?What evidence suggests gene therapy could treat inherited cardiomyopathies?
      Opinion onlyShould fast food be banned?How have menu-labeling or sodium policies affected blood pressure in U.S. adults?

      What your PBAT must include

      1. An essential questionAn analytical question about a mechanism or an impact. Not a yes/no question, and not a topic.
      2. Source synthesisAt least 3 peer-reviewed sources, combined in your argument and cited in APA 7 or MLA 9.
      3. Data analysisAt least one dataset, graph, or clinical study result that you interpret yourself.
      4. Visual evidenceA labeled diagram or model that shows the physiology behind your claim.
